[fact]
Ears tire in the highs first: after loud listening, sensitivity drops most around 4 kHz.
+3 dB halves the safe listening time. Small level changes matter.
WHO's safe limit for adults: about 80 dB(A) for 40 hours a week.
Ringing ears after a session is a warning sign, not a badge.
Dull ears after loud listening usually recover within hours. Repeated damage doesn't.
Damaged hair cells in the inner ear don't grow back.
Ears adapt to loudness within minutes. What sounds normal now may be loud.
Turning down by 6 dB cuts the sound energy by 75 %.
Noise-induced hearing loss is painless and gradual. You notice it late.
The ear's protective reflex tires with time and is too slow for sharp transients.
Mixes made late in long sessions tend to get brighter and louder.
After a few repeats, the brain stops listening to a loop critically.
Around 80 dB the ear's frequency response is flatter than at whisper levels.
A quiet room lets ears recover better than a noisy café.
Research suggests loud sound can harm the ear's nerve links before a hearing test shows it.
Headphones have no room to soften the sound. It's easy to listen louder than you think.
Bass needs far more energy to sound as loud as the mids. A-weighting discounts it for that.
A 10-minute break per hour is the classic studio rule.
Musicians' earplugs reduce level evenly instead of muffling. Use them at gigs.
Your hearing is the one tool in your studio you can't upgrade.
